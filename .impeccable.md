## Design Context

### Users
Jeremy — a working front-end developer preparing for the post-bac Traditional Chinese
Medicine (學士後中醫) entrance exam while holding a full-time job. Studies 4–6 hrs/day,
no cram school, frequently on **phone/tablet** in short sessions. Personal, password-gated
tool. Traditional Chinese (zh-Hant) throughout.

### Use cases
- Targeted review: filter past-exam questions by school / year / subject / concept-tag.
- Two drill modes: Study (instant answer + explanation, wrong-answer book) and Exam
  (hidden answers, score on submit).
- Analytics: concept-trend, cross-school weighting, weakness clusters, coverage.
- Concept notes: plain-language, must-memorize, solving-approach, TTS audio, example Qs.

### Brand Personality
Calm, focused, trustworthy study utility. Three words: **clear, efficient, dependable.**
Correctness is sacred (answers from official keys, never guessed). Not flashy — it should
get out of the way so the user can grind questions fast.

### Aesthetic Direction
Clean, legible, **mobile-first**, **light mode** (user-mandated for the app). DaisyUI + Tailwind.
The entry/landing is intentionally a dark, artsy "nonsense" gate (deliberate contrast — not
part of the app's working aesthetic). Anti-reference: generic AI dashboard.

### Design Principles
1. Legibility first — Chinese question text and chemistry images must read effortlessly on a phone.
2. Fast drilling — minimal clicks to answer/reveal; keyboard-friendly on desktop.
3. Trust signals — clearly mark errata-corrected and 待核對 answers.
4. Quiet by default, with personality reserved for moments (landing, reveal).
